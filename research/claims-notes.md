# Official product claims: MCAT tools and Math Academy

Research date: 2026-07-30

## Scope and evidentiary boundary

These notes cover **first-party product pages and help documentation only**. They are a claims map for later hands-on testing, not a record of observed in-product behavior. Claims about adaptation, representativeness, or readiness should be treated as hypotheses until tested. The three recommended MCAT products all advertise no-credit-card free access as of the research date:

1. Blueprint MCAT — 14-day trial
2. UWorld MCAT — 7-day trial
3. Khan Academy MCAT — free course

They make a useful test set because they expose three different progress models: exam-score analytics (Blueprint), performance reporting plus adaptive scheduling (UWorld), and course/skill coverage (Khan Academy).

## 1. Blueprint MCAT

### Free access and usable surfaces

- Blueprint advertises a 14-day, no-credit-card trial containing a half-length diagnostic, a complete full-length exam, and two practice sets. Trial users also receive flashcards, analytics, and a Lessons Learned Journal.
- The public free-resources page likewise promises practice questions, a half-length diagnostic, a full-length exam, and ten learning modules.

Sources:

- https://blueprintprep.com/mcat/practice-exams
- https://blueprintprep.com/mcat/free-resources/
- https://help.blueprintprep.com/en/articles/15363447-mcat-14-day-free-trial

### What level the product implies it measures

- Blueprint calls its diagnostic a “baseline score you can trust,” says it is “statistically equivalent to the real MCAT,” and reports an average difference of 0.3 points in its own representativeness study.
- It says its practice tests mirror the MCAT interface, question style, content scope, difficulty, and scoring.
- The marketing implication is therefore **MCAT performance/readiness**, not merely content recall.

### Likely DOK to verify hands-on

- Full-length, passage-based questions can plausibly reach **DOK 2–3**, depending on whether an item requires routine application or strategic evidence-based reasoning.
- Flashcards and discrete questions may remain **DOK 1–2**.
- A scaled test score may be useful performance evidence, but the public documentation does not establish that it measures **DOK 4 transfer/readiness**. “Representative” and “predictive of readiness” are claims to test, not automatic consequences of a full-length format.

### What its progress numbers claim to count

- The account analytics page defines “Scores Over Time” as Practice Exam or Section Exam scores plotted against a goal score.
- “Latest Completed Test” is the most recent practice exam and links to detailed analytics, including time on each question.
- Aggregate analytics combine full-length exams, section exams, and Qbank work over a selected date range.
- Breakdowns include correct/incorrect/unanswered, subject/category metadata, answer-change direction (right-to-wrong, wrong-to-right, wrong-to-wrong), bookmarks, section, subject, AAMC reasoning skill, and question type.
- The product page visually labels a Qbank practice set with a percentage “Performance” number. This appears to be answer performance, not a readiness probability.
- “Study progress” is also retained after trial expiration, but the public help article does not define its formula.

Source:

- https://help.blueprintprep.com/en/articles/9757728-mcat-lsat-account-analytics

### Adaptation, rewording, and uncertainty

- Blueprint says its AI Qbank “automatically serves up practice sets tailored to your performance.”
- The first-party pages reviewed do **not** say that a missed question is reworded and asked again.
- They do **not** document an explicit “the system does not know” or low-confidence state for recommendations, score interpretation, or topic diagnosis.
- The Lessons Learned Journal invites student reflection, but public claims do not show whether the platform elicits a pre-answer confidence judgment or computes calibration error.

Source:

- https://blueprintprep.com/mcat/online-course

### Calibration versus reassurance

- Strong calibration signals claimed: scaled exam score, goal-score comparison, timing per item, answer-change analysis, and weakness breakdowns.
- Reassurance language is also prominent: “baseline score you can trust,” “always know your next move,” and confidence that tests are representative.
- Hands-on test: determine whether results distinguish “insufficient evidence” from “weak topic,” and whether small samples are presented with unwarranted precision.

### Where volume may be sold as progress

- Blueprint prominently markets “5,000+” Qbank questions, “1,200 discrete and passage-based question sets,” and a schedule of exams.
- Its more diagnostic surfaces are score and category analytics; the risk to test is whether the planner/progress UI rewards completing modules and questions independently of performance or transfer.

### Timing

- Detailed exam analytics include timing for each question.
- The product recommends a cadence of practice exams and claims to mirror test-day interface and conditions.
- Hands-on test: inspect whether Qbank timing is benchmarked meaningfully, whether pauses/reviews distort timing, and whether pacing feedback changes recommendations.

## 2. UWorld MCAT

### Free access and usable surfaces

- UWorld advertises a 7-day, no-credit-card trial with a custom adaptive study plan, the first interactive lesson from every subject, videos, flashcards, performance reporting, and 100 representative Qbank questions with explanations.

Sources:

- https://gradschool.uworld.com/mcat/prep-course/
- https://gradschool.uworld.com/mcat/

### What level the product implies it measures

- UWorld says its Qbank provides “realistic MCAT questions,” its analytics provide “instant insight into MCAT exam readiness,” and peer benchmarks “confirm your readiness.”
- Its full-length exams use scaled 118–132 section scores and 472–528 totals; UWorld says its equating table yields realistic scores that “predict your MCAT readiness.”
- The implication is **performance and readiness**, extending well beyond lesson completion.

### Likely DOK to verify hands-on

- Passage-based Qbank and full-length simulations may measure **DOK 2–3**.
- Concept checks, flashcard quiz questions, and short lesson checks may be **DOK 1–2**.
- The public claim that topic analytics and peer comparison “confirm readiness” does not itself demonstrate **DOK 4 transfer**. Test whether readiness is inferred from repeated item-bank performance or from genuinely novel, integrated conditions.

### What its progress numbers claim to count

- Dynamic Study Planner: progress through weekly assignments; the schedule adjusts in real time.
- UBook Dashboard: completion status across digital books.
- Detailed Performance Reports: scores by subject and specific topic, trends over time, and peer benchmarks.
- Practice exams: scaled section and total scores intended to resemble MCAT scoring.
- Public pages conflate several distinct constructs under “progress”: schedule adherence, content completion, percent performance, topic weakness, peer rank, and exam score. The hands-on teardown should keep these separate.

Sources:

- https://gradschool.uworld.com/mcat/prep-course/
- https://gradschool.uworld.com/mcat/practice-test/

### Adaptation, rewording, and uncertainty

- The planner adapts to schedule and performance; performance reporting selects weak subjects/topics for attention.
- Spaced-repetition flashcards adapt review timing.
- The public pages reviewed do **not** state that the system rewords a question after an error.
- They do **not** disclose an explicit uncertainty or “not enough data” state in readiness, weakness, or peer-comparison reports.
- UAsk is described as an AI tutor trained by UWorld experts, but the reviewed materials do not specify confidence disclosure, refusal behavior, or whether it labels uncertainty.

### Calibration versus reassurance

- Potential calibration: raw-to-scaled exam reporting, trends, granular skill/subject breakdown, and peer benchmarks.
- Potential false reassurance to test: UWorld explicitly says benchmarking can “confirm readiness” and its explanations/course are framed as building confidence. Peer percentile and Qbank accuracy may not equal test-day readiness if practice was untimed, topic-filtered, or repeated.
- The official pages do not describe confidence judgments made by the learner before answering, so metacognitive calibration appears indirect.

### Where volume may be sold as progress

- UWorld prominently markets 3,000+ MCAT-level questions, 1,300+ videos, 4,000 flashcard quiz questions, eight books, and 450 concept checks.
- The UBook dashboard explicitly tracks completion, while the planner tracks assignments. Those are content-volume/plan-compliance measures, distinct from performance.
- Hands-on test: inspect whether the top-level dashboard visually privileges completed content over transfer, retention, and timed mixed performance.

### Timing

- UWorld says full-length tests recreate the interface and conditions so endurance and pacing are measured.
- Its diagnostic algorithm is not publicly documented for MCAT in the same detail as Math Academy’s.
- Hands-on test: determine whether ordinary Qbank analytics preserve time-per-item, distinguish tutor versus timed mode, penalize excessive time, or use timing in readiness estimates.

Sources:

- https://gradschool.uworld.com/mcat/practice-test/
- https://gradschool.uworld.com/mcat/mcat-prep-app/

## 3. Khan Academy MCAT

### Free access and usable surfaces

- The current MCAT course is free and contains 15 units and 518 listed skills.
- It includes four dedicated practice-passage units: Biological/Biochemical, Chemical/Physical, Psychological/Social/Biological, and CARS, followed by ten foundation/content units.
- Khan says the course continues in partnership with AAMC.

Source:

- https://www.khanacademy.org/test-prep/mcat

### What level the product implies it measures

- The MCAT landing page presents “free, trusted, high-quality MCAT study resources,” not an explicit score predictor.
- Its organization spans passage practice plus foundation lessons. It therefore implies MCAT preparation, but the reviewed official MCAT page does not claim that a course-progress percentage predicts an MCAT score or readiness.

### Likely DOK to verify hands-on

- Foundation videos and recall exercises are likely **DOK 1–2**.
- Passage sets can reach **DOK 2–3** if they require inference and integration.
- Course coverage across 518 skills is not itself DOK 4 readiness evidence.

### What its progress numbers claim to count

- Khan’s general Mastery System defines course mastery as percentage/fraction of available mastery points.
- Each skill is worth 100 points: Familiar = 50, Proficient = 80, Mastered = 100. Levels rise or fall based on completed exercises, quizzes, unit tests, and course challenges.
- It reports skills leveled up, down, or unchanged and may recommend lessons based on performance.
- Important caveat: the official list of mastery-enabled courses names math and several other courses but does not list MCAT. Hands-on testing must verify whether the MCAT’s visible “progress” is mastery points, activity completion, or another construct. Do not import the general mastery definition into MCAT without observing it.
- Energy points are a separate engagement currency: correct answers earn points and completing an activity earns extra points. They persist even if an activity is restarted, while mastery points do not change until completion.

Sources:

- https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery-
- https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360014675332-Which-courses-have-mastery-enabled-
- https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/9815463103245-What-happens-to-my-Mastery-and-Energy-points-when-I-start-an-activity-over

### Adaptation, rewording, and uncertainty

- General Khan mastery activities can recommend lessons from performance.
- Mastery Challenges are personalized and randomized, but Khan explicitly says they are currently for math courses; this should not be attributed to MCAT.
- The official sources reviewed do **not** establish that MCAT questions are reworded after an error.
- They do **not** describe an explicit “not enough evidence” state for MCAT progress or recommendations.

### Calibration versus reassurance

- Passage practice can provide answer-level feedback, but the reviewed official pages do not claim an MCAT score estimate, confidence interval, or readiness probability.
- This restraint may avoid a false predictive promise, but the interface could still let completion/skill counts feel like readiness. Hands-on testing should inspect the language surrounding the progress indicator.
- No official evidence found of pre-answer confidence ratings or calibration curves.

### Where volume may be sold as progress

- “15 units” and “518 skills” foreground curriculum volume.
- General Energy Points explicitly reward each correct answer and activity completion, making them engagement rather than readiness measures.
- Hands-on test: determine whether MCAT progress primarily counts watched/read/completed items and whether passage performance is separated from content consumption.

### Timing

- General Khan activity cards may show roughly how long other students took for quizzes, unit tests, and challenges.
- No first-party source reviewed establishes MCAT-specific timed simulation, pacing diagnosis, or timing-adjusted readiness.
- This is a key contrast with Blueprint and UWorld full-length exams.

## 4. Math Academy

### Access caveat

- Math Academy’s public site currently advertises $49/month per student and a “Join Beta” call to action. Unlike the three MCAT selections, free access is not clearly promised on the reviewed official pages. Account creation should verify whether a trial, diagnostic-only access, or payment is required before studying.

Source:

- https://www.mathacademy.com/

### What level the product implies it measures

- Math Academy says its adaptive diagnostic measures “mastery and automaticity,” identifies a student’s “knowledge frontier,” missing prerequisite knowledge, and topic-level mastery.
- It says successful completion of the Mathematical Foundations sequence prepares learners for upper-level university mathematics; Methods of Proof explicitly teaches formal argument.
- The core task engine appears primarily aimed at **procedural fluency and conceptual application (DOK 1–2)**, with some multistep/application work plausibly **DOK 2–3**. Proof construction and unfamiliar application could reach **DOK 3**, but that needs direct observation.
- Course completion as preparation for later coursework is not the same as measuring **DOK 4 transfer**.

Sources:

- https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works
- https://www.mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works
- https://www.mathacademy.com/courses/methods-of-proof

### What its progress number really claims to count

- Math Academy is unusually explicit: XP is earned for successfully completed lessons, reviews, multisteps, and quizzes, with the amount depending on performance.
- **One XP is “roughly equivalent to 1-minute of focused effort.”** Daily XP is therefore principally an effort/work-unit and pacing currency, not a probability of mastery or readiness.
- XP also rewards behaviors such as reading examples, using pencil and paper, checking incorrect answers against worked solutions, and perfect scores.
- Completing tasks updates the knowledge profile and unlocks advanced topics.
- Course estimates are calculated using different daily XP pacing assumptions.
- Separate from XP, the knowledge graph records levels of mastery and spaced-repetition stability for each topic. Darker graph nodes mean more stable knowledge through successful spaced repetitions.

Sources:

- https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works
- https://www.mathacademy.com/adult-students

### Diagnostic adaptation and whether it admits uncertainty

- The diagnostic adapts question selection to locate the boundary between known and unknown topics and considers prerequisite/postrequisite relationships.
- Math Academy explicitly describes handling conflicting evidence by weighting positive and negative observations.
- It also describes a meaningful uncertainty state: borderline topics may be marked **“conditionally completed.”** The system provisionally assumes knowledge, then “falls backwards” to prerequisite paths if later struggle supplies contrary evidence.
- Slow correct answers are weaker evidence than timely correct answers.
- This is the clearest first-party example among the four products of a system representing that its knowledge estimate is provisional rather than certain.

Source:

- https://www.mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works

### Does it reword questions?

- No reviewed official page says the system paraphrases/rewords the same question after an error.
- The described mechanism is different: it selects new tasks, schedules explicit prerequisite review, uses worked solutions, and offers quiz retakes.
- Hands-on test should distinguish parameter-swapped variants from genuinely reworded or structurally different transfer items.

### Calibration versus reassurance

- Calibration-oriented design claims include a 30–45 minute adaptive diagnostic, explicit topic-by-topic report, missing-foundation report, “conditionally completed” status, performance-sensitive evidence weighting, and timed quizzes.
- The platform also warns that diagnostic outcomes may be “unanticipated” and says an accurate map of current knowledge matters—more diagnostic than purely reassuring language.
- Risk to test: whether the UI explains uncertainty and evidence to the learner, or whether the sophistication exists only in the technical marketing page.

### Where content volume may be sold as progress

- Math Academy advertises thousands of linked topics and completion estimates.
- However, it defines XP as focused effort rather than calling XP mastery. This is a useful conceptual separation.
- The possible slippage is that unlocking topics and accumulating XP may still feel like “being mathematically ready,” while topic stability, timed performance, and novel transfer are separate.
- Hands-on test: compare dashboard prominence of daily XP, course completion percentage, knowledge stability, quiz performance, and estimated completion date.

### What it does about timing

- Timed quizzes appear after roughly every 150 XP.
- Math Academy says failure to answer within time means the skill is not yet mastered enough; incorrect or over-time topics trigger immediate review and a retake becomes available.
- Timing accommodations can be adjusted.
- Diagnostic evidence also discounts correct answers that take excessively long.
- This makes timing part of its mastery model rather than merely an analytics afterthought.

Sources:

- https://mathacademy.com/faq
- https://www.mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works

## Cross-product hypotheses for the hands-on teardown

| Product | Top-level construct claimed | What the visible number may actually count | DOK range most likely | Readiness overreach to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blueprint | Baseline, performance, score improvement | Exam score, percent correct, category performance, test/module completion | 1–3 by surface | Whether limited or repeated practice is presented with predictive certainty |
| UWorld | Knowledge gaps, improvement, peer-confirmed readiness | Assignment completion, book completion, Qbank accuracy, peer rank, exam score | 1–3 by surface | Whether untimed/topic-filtered accuracy is allowed to stand in for test readiness |
| Khan MCAT | MCAT learning/practice coverage | Likely activity/skill completion; exact MCAT formula must be observed | 1–3 by surface | Whether 518-skill/course completion visually implies readiness without score evidence |
| Math Academy | Mastery, automaticity, knowledge frontier | XP ≈ focused minutes; separate topic stability and timed mastery signals | 1–3 by task/course | Whether procedural/topic mastery is generalized to broad mathematical transfer |

## Concrete observation protocol

For each account, capture exact on-screen copy and screenshots at these moments:

1. Before any work: every baseline/progress/readiness number and its tooltip.
2. Submit a confident correct response, a slow correct response, a confident wrong response, and an “I don’t know”/skip if available.
3. Repeat the same skill until a question recurs; record whether it is identical, parameter-swapped, paraphrased, or structurally novel.
4. Compare untimed/tutor and timed modes on the same topic.
5. Open every analytics breakdown and record numerator, denominator, sample size, date window, excluded attempts, and whether confidence/uncertainty is shown.
6. Trigger a recommendation after sparse evidence and after contradictory evidence.
7. Log whether consuming a video/lesson raises the headline progress number without demonstrated performance.
8. Record whether timing affects only a report, changes mastery, or changes the next task.

The final analysis should label each cited statement as either **Observed behavior** or **Official claim**. Do not use marketing copy as evidence that the product actually behaves as claimed.
